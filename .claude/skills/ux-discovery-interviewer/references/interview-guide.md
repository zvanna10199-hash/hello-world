# Interview Guide

## 1. Primary Discovery Lenses

**User**
- Who is the primary user?
- Who else affects or approves the flow?
- What skill level or domain knowledge do they have?

**Goal**
- What outcome are they trying to achieve?
- What does success look like from the user's perspective?
- What matters more: speed, confidence, accuracy, convenience, visibility, collaboration?

**Trigger and Context**
- What event causes the need to appear?
- How often does this happen?
- In what setting does it happen?
- What constraints exist: time, device, policy, risk, interruptions?

**Current Behavior**
- What does the user do today?
- What tools, manual steps, or workarounds are involved?
- What is annoying, slow, risky, or confusing?

**Journey**
Map the experience across these stages when relevant:
1. trigger
2. discover options
3. start task
4. complete key actions
5. confirm result
6. handle failure or follow-up

**Value and Opportunity**
- What would make the biggest difference for the user?
- Which pain points are severe, frequent, or expensive?
- Which opportunities are incremental vs transformative?

## 2. Interview Pacing

Prefer this rhythm:
1. summarize current understanding
2. ask 3 to 5 focused questions
3. synthesize
4. continue only where uncertainty is highest

Extract and structure lengthy answers before asking additional questions.

## 3. Inference Rules

Categorize findings clearly:
- **Known**: directly stated by the user
- **Inferred**: reasonable interpretation from statements
- **Unknown**: missing information requiring investigation

## 4. Follow-up Request Update Method

### Update Checklist
- What new fact was introduced?
- Does it change the target user?
- Does it change the user goal?
- Does it change the happy path?
- Does it reveal new pain points or edge cases?
- Does it create new opportunities?
- What questions does it open?

### Suggested Response Shape
Include: What changed, What stays the same, Updated pain points/opportunities, Updated journey, Next questions (3-5 bullets)

## 5. Quality Bar

A strong synthesis addresses:
- who are we designing for?
- what are they trying to accomplish?
- what is hard today?
- what is the ideal flow?
- where are the best product opportunities?
- what do we still need to learn?
